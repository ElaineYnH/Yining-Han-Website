Blog 9 rubric evidence and verification record

This is an evidence checklist, not a promise of a particular grade.

COMMON CRITERIA
1. Question/motivation: opening asks whether reducing forecast error improves an
   investment strategy. The discrepancy between forecast and portfolio results
   answers a concrete question relevant to evaluating stock-prediction models.
2. Explanation: the article explains boosting, RMSE, the forecast benchmark,
   chronology, the original forward target, portfolio formation and costs.
3. Interpretation/takeaway: the revision reduces RMSE relative to the class model,
   fails to beat a training-mean forecast and earns less than the class portfolio.
   Its small net advantage over SPY at 10bps comes with worse Sharpe/drawdown and
   disappears at 25bps. No causal or future-return claim is made.
4. Data/presentation: source files, manifest, dates, sample counts and SHA-256
   checks are retained. Two PNG figures and a risk/return table directly support
   the same question. Units, return windows, costs and the Sharpe convention
   are labeled. Separate smaller-screen figures avoid compressed text.
5. Organization: code/, data/, results/ and README.md with exact replication and
   website installation commands. The sole article is blog9.qmd. Notes and
   templates use .txt, not additional renderable Markdown files.
6. Reproducibility: full Python build actually completed. It regenerates the
   article, forecasts, validation results, models, weights, daily equity curves,
   metrics and figures from the unchanged real-data snapshot. Parameters and
   evaluation rules were fixed before the first test run. Independent checks run.

BLOG 9 CRITERIA
7. Original experiment: article section 1, original_class_model.R and the mapping
   audit explain the provided classroom model and original signals, universe,
   target and portfolio. Mixed classroom balances are contextual, not presented
   as controlled XGBoost evidence. The article discusses noisy/changing returns,
   overfitting, correlated stocks and overlapping target windows.
8. Substantive revision: 21 scale-free/ranked predictors, shallower trees,
   penalties and 12 hyperparameter combinations. Three chronological, purged
   2023 validation windows select depth2, 150trees, learning_rate0.03, lambda20.
9. Out-of-sample comparison: common purged pre-2024 training sample; 66,120 test
   forecasts across 696 dates. Both the classroom specification and revised
   model are compared to a constant training-mean forecast, with an additional
   zero-return sensitivity. Test data never select parameters or refit models.
10. Predictive/economic performance: RMSE, MAE, OOS R-squared, direction accuracy
    and daily rank correlation are saved. Common daily, self-financing portfolios
    with identical execution dates compare both models, SPY buy-and-hold and
    monthly equal weighting of the 95-stock universe. Gross, 10bps and 25bps
    cases include entry/exit costs. Net CAGR, daily Sharpe and max drawdown
    show why improved forecast error does not establish investment usefulness.
