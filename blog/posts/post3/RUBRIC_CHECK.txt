Revision
Question: Had the education gap in jobholding closed after unemployment
fell from its pandemic level?
Figure 1 establishes the unequal unemployment shock and subsequent decline.
Figure 2 shows that recovery toward each group's own baseline coexists
with a 24.8-point gap in employment in 2025 (26.6 points in 2019).
Figure 3 places approximately 22.9 points of the 2025 gap in nonparticipation
and 2.0 points in unemployment, using the same population denominator.
About 92% corresponds to nonparticipation. This is an accounting result;
the analysis does not identify causes or reasons for nonparticipation.
The BLS definition explains why people outside the labor force should not
all be treated as unsuccessful jobseekers. Participation rates are stated
explicitly, and the conclusion connects all three figures.

5. Organization: code/, data/, results/, figures/, and this README's actual

6. Reproducibility: build_from_summary.R regenerates figures, article numbers,
   and saved decomposition/metrics, with arithmetic consistency checks.
7. IPUMS CPS microdata: original preparation script and its weighted output
   are retained. Private individual records are not redistributed.
8. Appropriate weights: prepare_cps.R uses the basic monthly person weight
   WTFINL for every numerator and denominator. The revision verifies all
   three rate formulas against the saved weighted totals.
9. At least three substantive figures: unemployment over time, employment
   over time, and nonemployment status composition in 2025.
10. Coherent story: shock eased -> jobholding rebounded but gap persisted ->
    most of the current gap corresponds to nonparticipation. The decomposition
    connects the figures without implying a causal education effect.
