COMMON CRITERIA

1. Question and motivation
   Opening: can ML beat a credible weekly rule for next-day hourly bike rentals?
   Practical use: planning busy service hours. System-level completed rentals
   are distinguished from unmet demand and station inventory needs.

2. Clear explanation
   Plain-language descriptions of forest averaging, sequential boosting, the
   day-ahead information set, chronological validation, MAE, and RMSE.
   Main narrative: approximately 654 words excluding headings, figures/captions,
   the score table, and the collapsed replication/source note.

3. Interpretation and takeaway
   Held-out MAE falls from 69.5 to 55.3 rentals/hour, about 20%. The narrative
   includes peak-hour errors and a large Sunday miss in the fixed example
   week. The chosen model follows earlier validation, where its advantage was
   small; the article does not claim lasting or current operational superiority.

4. Data and presentation
   Official UCI source, dataset DOI, source study and attribution in article
   and README. Original source CSVs and documentation included unmodified.
   Three distinct figures: model error, a fixed test-week prediction example,
   and grouped permutation importance. Desktop and mobile figure versions
   have legible labels, units, explicit periods, captions and alt text.

5. Repository organization
   Separate code/, data/raw/, figures/ and results/, plus README.txt with exact
   publishing and replication steps. All article resource links resolve in
   the rendered site. Internal notes are .txt to avoid extra listed articles.

6. Reproducibility and coding
   build_blog.py implements ingestion, exact calendar lags, training-fold
   preprocessing, tuning, selection, test predictions, scores, interpretation,
   figures and article generation. Numeric values are substituted from saved
   results. Fixed seed, package versions and complete raw inputs included.
   Independent checks are supplied in audit_workflow.py.

POST 10 CRITERIA

7. Two machine-learning algorithms plus a simple benchmark
   RandomForestRegressor and HistGradientBoostingRegressor, plus training-only
   hour/workday means and the stronger exact t-168h weekly forecast.
   All four algorithms/rules are evaluated on identical held-out observations.

8. Proper train/test procedure and tuning without test data
   Test reserved: October–December 2012, 2,101 recorded hours with usable lags.
   Three expanding validation windows cover the preceding three quarters.
   Eight forest and twelve boosting parameter sets; training-fold one-hot
   encoding and disabled random internal early stopping. Model selection JSON
   is written before test scores are calculated. Only forecast-time-available
   inputs are used. No target components or future observed weather.

9. Appropriate out-of-sample evaluation
   MAE is the prespecified selection/primary score; RMSE describes larger misses.
   Bias, every test prediction, fold scores, and errors by hour are saved.
   Working-day 7–9 a.m. and 4–7 p.m. errors supplement the aggregate results.

10. Interpretation and reason for model choice
   Twenty grouped input permutations per group explain model reliance after
   selection; correlated demand measures are shuffled together. The article
   distinguishes predictive reliance from causal effects and warns about
   unusual predictor combinations. Random forest is chosen by validation MAE.
