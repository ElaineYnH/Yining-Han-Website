**Can the apparent link between parents' and children's incomes change simply because we measure earnings at different ages?** This mini-tutorial uses SQL inside Python to match family members and compare four income measures. The lesson is that correct matching is only the beginning: the income window also matters.

We use **simulated teaching data**, with @@N@@ families and one designated parent–child pair per family. `people.csv` contains stable person IDs, origin-family IDs, birth years, and an explicit parent ID. `earnings.csv` contains @@ROWS@@ person-year records. ## 4. Estimate and interpret the elasticity

The **intergenerational elasticity (IGE)** is the slope from regressing log child income on log parent income, with an intercept. For example, an IGE near 0.5 associates a 10% parental-income difference with roughly a 5% child-income difference. It is an association, not the fraction of income inherited or a causal effect.

Holding the single-year parental measure fixed, moving child income from age 25 to 40 raises the estimate from **@@YOUNG_ONE@@ to @@MID_ONE@@**. In this model, future higher earners have faster career growth, so early income differences understate long-run differences. Reducing everybody's income by the same percentage would shift the log-income intercept, not the elasticity. This illustrates the age-dependent proxy problem studied by [Haider and Solon (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.96.4.1308).

Five-year averages at midcareer produce **@@MID_MEAN@@**, nearer the @@TARGET@@ benchmark. Averaging reduces transitory variation, including noise in parental income that can attenuate the slope. Yet averaging young incomes still gives only **@@YOUNG_MEAN@@**: smoothing several early years does not remove their compressed income gaps. These conclusions reflect the stated simulation, not a claim about the magnitude of real-world bias.

**Takeaway:** Match on verified relationships, aggregate within person, and compare income windows on the same pairs. Longer averages can better approximate long-run status, but observation age remains essential. With real data, also justify the treatment of zero incomes, missing years, inflation, and sample selection.
