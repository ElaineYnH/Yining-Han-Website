Format
A compact tutorial: task, actual short Python/SQL code, generated output,
explanation, and one reveal-answer checkpoint. Approximately 600 words of
prose, excluding code, tables, figure, and source footer; one substantive
figure. Blog 6 does not impose Blog 3/4's three-figure requirement.

Six common criteria
1. Question and motivation: opening asks whether observing the same families
   at different ages can change their apparent income association.
2. Clear explanation: four numbered steps explain loading, the relationship
   join, per-person aggregation, and the log-log elasticity. A short example
   gives the percent-change interpretation.
3. Interpretation and takeaway: contrasts young vs. midcareer estimates,
   then single-year vs. five-year measures, on a common set of pairs. Explains
   why averaging early-career income can leave life-cycle bias.
4. Data and presentation: clearly labeled simulated data, fixed seed,
   constant-dollar units, input schema, a known assumed benchmark, generated
   tables and figure, and primary methodological/documentation links.
5. Repository organization: code/, data/, results/, requirements, and README
   with actual commands and installation/publication instructions.
6. Reproducibility: the lesson runs SQL, saves matched incomes and estimates,
   and inserts the executed code and computed numbers into a static article.
   The generator and all inputs are included for an offline full rebuild.

Four Blog 6 criteria
7. SQL in Python and identifiers: sqlite3 + pandas; a self-join uses explicit
   parent_id/person_id links and checks origin-family IDs. Repeated person-
   year observations supply longitudinal incomes. The panel is simulated.
8. SELECT/JOIN/GROUP BY/aggregation: explained alongside the actual working
   queries; AVG, WHERE, HAVING, and bound parameters are also explained.
   The checkpoint demonstrates the 4,000-row family-only join error.
9. Income measures and IGE: six constructed income measures, four IGE
   estimates using single-year and five-year income, intercept included,
   levels averaged before logs, same 1,000 pairs in every comparison.
10. Life-cycle bias and averaging: known generating beta 0.50, early-career
    gaps deliberately compressed, young vs. midcareer comparison with the
    parental single-year measure held fixed. Five-year averages reduce
    transitory variation but leave the early-career compression. The text
    does not equate every change in an empirical estimate with proof of bias.

Evidence boundaries
No real-world income estimates, survey microdata, population weighting,
statistical significance, or causal education/income effects are claimed.
The model is a teaching illustration with disclosed assumptions; the stated
0.50 is a generating parameter, not a national empirical benchmark.
This checklist documents evidence; it is not a prediction or guarantee of
a grade.
