SIX COMMON ITEMS

1. Question and motivation
   Opening question asks whether measuring child earnings in the twenties or
   thirties changes the parent-child association. The result paragraph shows
   why measurement choices change the apparent strength of income persistence.
   A single-year paternal IGE of 0.055/0.118 becomes 0.274/0.323 using averages.

2. Clear explanation
   Four numbered steps, five consecutive runnable Python blocks, plain-language
   explanations of SQL and income ownership, and a reveal-answer exercise.
   Explains arithmetic means before logs and the percentage interpretation of IGE.

3. Interpretation and takeaway
   Interprets the eight actual estimates for the same 606 children. Separates
   maternal/paternal associations and avoids causal claims. Reports the paired
   age-difference interval, including zero; does not label the older slope true.
   Ends with concrete advice: aggregate first, use several observations at
   economically comparable ages, and report ages and the selected sample.

4. Data and presentation
   Uses the actual user-supplied PSID J366685.zip public-use extract and BLS CPI.
   README/source manifest identify date, exact variables, wave/role/year rules,
   source links and ZIP fingerprint. Figure has age windows, IGE axis, numeric
   labels, parent-specific panels, single/average legend and bootstrap intervals.
   Figure note documents source, parent window, cluster count and 2,000 draws.

5. Repository organization
   code/, data/ and results/ with root README and dependencies. The README gives
   exact Windows commands and cross-platform equivalents. Individual PSID data
   are held outside the repository; each replicator registers and obtains their
   own extract. The required variable manifest permits reconstructing the input.
   Notes are .txt so they do not create extra Quarto blog entries.

6. Reproducibility and coding practices
   Actual fixed-width input read from .do positions. SQL constructs links and
   aggregates income; NumPy estimates slopes. Seeded bootstrap, result CSV/JSON,
   figure and article are generated programmatically. Wrapper was actually run
   end-to-end from a fresh private directory. Five displayed blocks were executed
   in order and agree with saved estimates. Data access remains required to
   independently replicate; the package does not redistribute individual records.

FOUR POST 6 ITEMS

7. SQL within Python, individual/family identifiers, parent-child matching
   tutorial.py: match_families uses sqlite3 and a CTE with mother and father
   identifiers, UNION ALL, and JOIN on original family identifier AND person
   number. Unknown/unobserved-parent codes are excluded. IDs combine id68 and pn
   only for subsequent convenience; family ID alone never asserts parenthood.
   Matching agrees with a separately computed pandas merge of both identifiers.

8. SELECT, JOIN, GROUP BY and aggregation
   Steps 2/3 explicitly explain SELECT, JOIN ON, WHERE, GROUP BY, AVG, COUNT and
   HAVING. A 4-by-3 annual-record exercise demonstrates the duplication problem.
   SQL averages/counts and nearest-age anchors agree with independent pandas
   calculations. Each role contributes one parent-child row after aggregation.

9. Construct incomes, single-year/multi-year IGE
   prepare_data.py assigns individual labor earnings by actual reference/spouse
   role and sequence, uses income-year age, valid special codes, released
   imputations and CPI-adjusted dollars. Parent window 35-44; child 20-29 and
   30-39; at least three observed income years. Singles nearest ages 40/25/35,
   earlier-year tie break. Means retain eligible zeros; positive log inputs and
   both-parent coverage keep the same 606 children across all eight fits. OLS
   includes an intercept; the independent least-squares calculation agrees.

10. Life-cycle bias and why averaging helps
   Final section gives an age-dependent loading equation. Under the stated
   assumptions the measured slope is lambda_t times the lifetime IGE, so a
   loading different from one biases the measured elasticity. A clearly labeled
   hypothetical parameter illustration makes the difference numerical; it is
   not simulated PSID data. Cites Haider and Solon (2006) for the empirical basis
   of age-dependent current/lifetime earnings relationships. The actual PSID
   age-window comparison illustrates sensitivity but cannot identify the true
   lifetime slope or isolate age from period effects. Explains parent-X noise
   reduction, child-income smoothing and the limits of twenties-only averages.
